Bias and misinformation

Bias and misinformation in generative AI

Generative AI is an incredibly useful tool, but it requires human intervention to ensure that bias and misinformation does not impact how outputs are used. Mindfulness and awareness about the data AI is trained on can help you determine whether an AI output is accurate or truthful.

 

What is WEIRD data? 

The acronym “WEIRD”—Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic—helps us understand how the quality of data can impact generative AI outputs. It is a framework developed by researchers to describe the most commonly studied demographics in behavior sciences and also applies to the kind of data that AI is trained on. When AI is trained primarily on data that falls within some or all of these categories, it can produce biased outputs that might:

  • Prioritize Western understandings of law, design, medicine or more subjective topics like morality or religion;
  • Fail to examine or integrate culturally relevant details in text, image or data; or
  • Reproduce nuanced stereotypes that reinforce harmful ideologies or beliefs.


Why it matters for higher education

AI has the capacity to dramatically change the landscape of teaching and learning and student support. However, if used without human intervention, cultural awareness or caution, it can lead to problems later down the road, whether it’s a critical student experience issue or grading feedback that lands flat.

Using RACCCA to evaluate AI outputs

Developed by ASU's Andrew Maynard, the RACCCA (Relevance, Accuracy, Completeness, Clarity, Coherence and Appropriateness) framework provides a simple way to determine whether an AI output followed the prompt you put in. It’s important to assess the quality of AI outputs to help you understand what AI can and can’t do for you, and how to make it work better.

Relevance

Does the AI-generated content address the specific task, question or goal in your prompt?

What to look for:

  • The AI response stays on-topic
  • The response addresses all parts of the prompt
  • Usefulness for the context of the prompt (business, casual, educational, etc.)
Accuracy

Is the information factual and correct based on available evidence or known sources?

What to look for:

  • Misinformation, disinformation or bias
  • Unverifiable information or statistics
  • Authoritative but incorrect language or statements

 BEST PRACTICE: Take time to research or verify AI outputs with reputable sources, especially if you are not a subject matter expert or do not have one available to fact-check AI’s work.

Completeness

Does the response cover all important aspects of the topic?

What to look for:

  • Any missing information or perspectives
  • Superficial coverage of the topic in the prompt
  • Missing prompt details left unaddressed
Clarity

Is the output easy to understand and well-expressed?

What to look for:

  • Tone of language and appropriateness for the context or audience (work, home, school, colleagues, friends, etc.)
  • Language and description of technical details or complex ideas 
  • Ease of understanding—would a non-expert be able to understand it? 
Coherence

Does the response make logical sense and flow well?

What to look for:

    • Logically ordered ideas in response to the prompt
    • Non-sensical language or expressions
    • Contradictions or non-sequiturs 
A non sequitur is a conclusion or statement that does not logically follow or make sense based on previous arguments or statements made in a discussion.
An example of a non sequitur: "I love writing, so I think I will go to the convenience store down the street."
Appropriateness

Is the tone, format and content suitable for the audience and context?

What to look for:

  • Tone matches the audience and sensitivity of the content or subject
  • Format of the response fits what was prompted
  • Bias, misrepresentation or stereotyping in the content

 BEST PRACTICE: Pause and reflect on blind spots in your own thinking or in the AI’s response to ensure you're thinking about how WEIRD data might impact outputs!

Using this framework helps you engage in a critical feedback loop with generative AI tools to improve not only how the AI responds but also how you put information in.
